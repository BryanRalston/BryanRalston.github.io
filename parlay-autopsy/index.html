      Feature Map — what this page does today

      Current capabilities. Not a roadmap. The autopsies stay on this device.

      • The slip Two to six legs. A name is required on each leg you keep. American odds are optional, like -110, +180, EVEN, or EV. Leave the odds box empty for a name only. Unsigned 180 is read as +180, and the odds box says so. EVEN and EV are +100. A unicode minus counts. Prices from 100 through 100000 are read. Anything else, including a percent, a fraction, or digits split by a space, asks you to fix the odds and does not cut. Mark the leg or legs that killed it. At least one kill mark is required. A note is optional. Whitespace around a price is trimmed. Names match with Unicode NFC and lowercase, so accents, non-Latin letters, and emoji stay. Word order inside a name does not matter. A signed number and its decimal stay one token, and a trailing plus stays, so Chiefs -3.5, Chiefs +3.5, Over 48.5, and Over 5.48 are different legs. Markup stays text.
      • Autopsy A theatrical AUTOPSY card. A cause-of-death stamp, one roast line, a tag, a corpse meter, and the legs. DEAD is the leg you marked. CASHED is the rest of the story you told. The page does not know the result. The first roast follows the slip, so the same legs and killers keep their line. A line is used only when it fits: one killer, the killer in that spot, or several killers. The corpse meter follows the share of legs that died, plus a small wobble and a long-shot bump. More dead legs do not lower it. It is entertainment theater, not a probability and not a model.
      • The stamp LAST LEG, MIDDLE BLEED, TOTAL COLLAPSE, NEAR MISS, or ONE-TICK. It is seeded from the legs you typed the first time. Every leg dead is TOTAL COLLAPSE. Exactly one killer whose American price sits from -130 through -100 or +100 through +130 is ONE-TICK, even if that leg is last. A cashed -110 does not move the stamp. NEAR MISS is exactly one killer in the first spot, outside that juice band. It is not used when two or more legs died. Exactly one killer in the last spot, outside that juice band, is LAST LEG. Exactly one killer in the middle is MIDDLE BLEED. Two or more killers, with the last leg among them, is LAST LEG. That includes all but one dead when the last leg died. Two or more killers while the last leg cashed is MIDDLE BLEED. That includes all but one dead when the last leg cashed. The corpse meter follows the share of legs that died, plus a small wobble and a long-shot bump, so more dead legs do not lower it for the same leg count. It is entertainment, not a probability.
      • Same slip Legs are order-normalized for the match. Names keep signed numbers and decimals whole, then the words are lowercased and sorted, then the legs themselves are sorted with their odds and kill marks. The same legs and the same killers share one card, including a re-autopsied roast. The first typed order keeps the stamp. A later reorder reopens that card and can save a new note. The form keeps what you just typed. The note does not move the stamp. A different killer, a different price, or the other side of a spread is a different corpse.
      • Re-autopsy A new roast on that same stamp. It does not always open with the same phrase. The tag stays with the stamp. The corpse meter stays. The star stays. Undo re-autopsy restores the previous line.
      • Star Mark an autopsy you want on the starred filter. Undo star takes the mark off.
      • Undo The Undo button takes back the last autopsy, re-autopsy, or star on the open card. Remove and Clear all use the toast Undo until it leaves. So does an autopsy that had to make room. That room Undo leaves once the new card is starred or re-autopsied. The toast Undo leaves as soon as it can no longer work, including after Load sample or a new autopsy changes the shelf, and when the cap would block a restore. A confirmation such as Link copied still shows. A form error stays on the form and does not cover a live Undo.
      • History Up to 24 autopsies on this phone. Filters are all and starred. Open one, remove one, or clear every autopsy. Opening a row does not replace what you are typing. Undo puts them back until the toast leaves. A new autopsy at the cap drops the oldest unstarred autopsy. Starred autopsies stay. If every autopsy is starred, the slab refuses another.
      • Snapshot share Copy link copies the open autopsy into #a= in one tap. Copy shelf link packs every autopsy. ?a= works if the token is in the query string. The link does not carry the star. Opening it offers Keep or Not now. Keep recomputes the stamp, tag, meter, and roast from the legs, so a forged line does not survive. A shelf link keeps what fits. The offer says what it adds, and how many oldest unstarred autopsies it drops, before you tap. If nothing fits, the offer stays up. A duplicate says Already on this phone. A keep that finds some already here says how many were new and how many were already here. An empty #a= is cleared. The address bar is cleared on purpose after a real token too. A bad token toasts. A same-tab hash change shows the offer. It is not live sync. Nothing is uploaded.
      • Load sample Bills moneyline -150, Over 48.5 -110, and Mahomes anytime TD +180 as the killer, so there is a LAST LEG card to look at without typing.
      • On this device Store parlay-autopsy-v1. Offline cache parlay-autopsy-v3. The page is fetched from the network when you are online. Scripts and styles carry ?v= and are cached by the full URL, so a new version does not match an old one. If storage is blocked, a banner says so. If a save cannot be read, including a card that fails validation, the old value is backed up and a notice shows. No accounts, no odds feed, no analytics.
